Life in the UK Test: format, chapters and how to prepare

The Life in the UK Test is the test most people must pass when they apply for British citizenship or for settlement (indefinite leave to remain). This page explains what it asks, what it covers, and how to get ready for it — including how the free offline practice app on this site fits in.

The test at a glance

  • 24 questions, multiple choice, in 45 minutes.
  • 18 correct out of 24 to pass — 75%. Some questions ask for two answers, and you get the mark only if both are right.
  • Everything comes from the official handbook, Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents.
  • You book it through GOV.UK and sit it at an approved test centre with photo ID.
  • You are told the result the same day, and you can book again if you do not pass — a fee is payable each time.

What the test covers

The handbook has five chapters, and the test draws on all of them:

  1. The values and principles of the UK — the fundamental principles of British life, your rights and responsibilities, and the citizenship pledge.
  2. What is the UK? — the four nations and their capitals, the patron saints, the Union Flag, population, languages, currency and the physical geography.
  3. A long and illustrious history — from the Stone Age and the Romans through the Tudors and Stuarts, the Industrial Revolution, the two world wars and the modern era.
  4. A modern, thriving society — sport, music, art, architecture, literature, cinema, religion, celebrations and everyday life.
  5. The UK government, the law and your role — how Parliament and the courts work, devolution, the monarchy, your rights and responsibilities, and how to take part in your community.

You can read every fact behind these chapters in the revision notes.

How to prepare

  1. Read a chapter of the handbook first. The test is a test of the book, so nothing replaces reading it once through.
  2. Drill that chapter straight away. Answering questions on the chapter you have just read is what moves facts into memory.
  3. Sit timed mock tests. 24 questions in 45 minutes, with the same chapter mix as the real test. Aim for 22 or more out of 24, so that a bad day still passes.
  4. Flag what you keep missing and come back to it, rather than re-reading everything.
  5. Follow a plan that ends at your test date. A 4-week, 2-week or 1-week schedule beats cramming in the last three days.

Questions people ask

How many questions are in the Life in the UK Test?

24 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes, with 18 correct needed to pass (75%). Some questions ask for two answers.

What does the Life in the UK Test cover?

The five chapters of the official handbook, listed above: values and principles, what the UK is, history, modern society, and government and law.

Does this app contain the official test questions?

No. The questions here are original, written from public facts, and are not copied from the handbook or from any test provider. This is an independent study aid; always treat the official handbook as the source of truth.

Can I practise offline and for free?

Yes. It costs nothing, needs no account, and works with no connection after the first visit. Your progress is kept in your own browser.

Before you book

Rules, fees and exemptions change, and some people do not have to take the test at all. Check the current requirements on GOV.UK before you pay for anything.
