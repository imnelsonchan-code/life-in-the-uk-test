# 4-Week Life in the UK Test Plan

> **This file is the 4-week plan.** The app also ships **2-week** and **1-week**
> versions of the same schedule — see *The shorter plans* at the end.

**Your setup:** 1–2 hours per day · test in about 4 weeks · you own the official book,
the official *Practice Questions & Answers*, and the official app.

**The idea:** read a chapter section in the official handbook, immediately test it with
the official questions, then drill it in *this* app until it sticks. Read once, practise
three times — that is what moves facts into memory.

---

## How each resource is used

| Resource | Its job in this plan | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Official handbook** (*A Guide for New Residents*) | The reading — the source of truth | The test is based on it, word for word |
| **Official Practice Questions & Answers** | Chapter-by-chapter checking | Authoritative wording, matches the real style |
| **Official app** | Filling dead time (commute, queues) | Most convenient place to do official questions |
| **This app** | Volume drilling, mock tests, weak-area tracking | Unlimited questions, timer, stats, retry-wrong |

> **Rule of the plan:** never memorise *my* questions. Learn the *facts*. The official
> wording will be different on the day, and the facts are what are tested.

---

## The daily rhythm (about 1.5 hours)

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 10 min | Clear yesterday's wrong answers → **Practice → Retry wrong** |
| 45–55 min | Read today's handbook section and write short notes (dates, names, numbers) |
| 25–35 min | Practise: today's official questions + the app drills below |
| 5 min | Log the score. Flag anything you got wrong twice (the ⚐ on the question), then review it under **Practice → Review flagged** |

Keep a **single sheet of "key facts"** (dates, names, numbers) and add to it daily.
On the final days you re-read only that sheet — not the whole book.

## Week 1 — Foundations and early History

| Day | Focus | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Handbook **Chapter 1** — Values & Principles | Read Ch 1. Note the 5 fundamental principles, your rights & responsibilities, and the citizenship pledge — and read the practical half too (becoming a permanent resident, taking the test). Answer all Ch 1 official questions. **App: 20 × Values & Principles.** |
| **2** | **Chapter 2** — What is the UK? (nations) | Read: the 4 nations, capitals, Great Britain vs the UK, the British Isles, Crown dependencies, overseas territories. Note what "Great Britain" *excludes*. **App: 25 × What is the UK?** |
| **3** | **Chapter 2** — symbols & people | Read: patron saints & their days, national flowers, the Union Flag, population, languages, cities, currency — and the geography (hills, mountains, rivers, lakes). Answer all Ch 2 official questions. **App: 25 × What is the UK? + one Quick 10.** |
| **4** | **Chapter 3** — Early Britain | Read Stone Age → Romans → Anglo-Saxons → Vikings → Norman Conquest. Memorise the anchor dates: **AD 43, AD 410, 1066, 1086**. **App: 30 × History — set the Difficulty chip to Easy first.** |
| **5** | **Chapter 3** — The Middle Ages | Read: Magna Carta, Wales & Scotland, the Hundred Years War, the Black Death, the Wars of the Roses. Timeline card: **1215, 1314, 1348, 1415, 1455 (the Wars of the Roses begin), 1485**. **App: 30 × History.** |
| **6** | **Chapter 3** — The Tudors and Stuarts | Read: Henry VIII, the Reformation, Elizabeth I, the Spanish Armada, the Gunpowder Plot, the Civil War, the Restoration. Do the first half of Ch 3 official questions. **App: 30 × History.** |
| **7** | **Review + baseline mock** | Clear **every** wrong answer so far (Practice → Retry wrong). Sit the **baseline mock** (target ~15/24) — a starting point, not a verdict. Re-read your Ch 1–2 notes and your timeline card. |

**Milestone after Week 1:** mock score around **15/24**. If lower, that is normal at this stage.

## Week 2 — History (the biggest chapter)

| Day | Focus | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **8** | **Chapter 3** — 18th & 19th centuries | Read: the 1707 Union, the empire and the abolition of slavery, Trafalgar, Waterloo, the Reform Acts, Queen Victoria, the Industrial Revolution, Ireland. **App: 30 × History.** |
| **9** | **Chapter 3** — The 20th century (1) | Read: WWI and the Somme, women's suffrage (1918, 1928), Ireland, the Depression, WWII and the Battle of Britain. **App: 30 × History.** |
| **10** | **Chapter 3** — 20th century (2) & since 1945 | Read: the NHS (1948), the welfare state, the Commonwealth, the 1960s–2000s, broadcasting (the BBC was founded in 1922), devolution, and the UK leaving the EU in 2020. Do the second half of Ch 3 official questions. **App: 30 × History.** |
| **11** | **History consolidation** | **App: 30 × History — set the Difficulty chip to Hard first.** Review every wrong answer and add the fact to your notes. |
| **12** | **Weak areas + mock** | Check **Stats** and drill your two weakest categories. Sit **Mock #1** (**App → Mock**). Target **18+/24**. Review every wrong answer fully. |
| **13** | **Revisit the shorter chapters** | Retry your wrong answers — start with Values & Principles and What is the UK? (Practice → Retry wrong). Two Quick 10 sets. |
| **14** | **Rest + light revision** | Official **app** quiz only — 15–20 min. Re-read your key-facts sheet. No heavy study. |

**Milestone after Week 2:** mock score **18+/24** (this is the real pass mark).

## Week 4 — Exam readiness

| Day | Focus | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **22** | **Weakest areas** | **Stats** tab: find your two weakest categories and drill them (hard difficulty). Redo those chapters in the official Q&A. |
| **23** | **Sweep: Ch 1 + Ch 2** | Practise **all** Values & Principles (72) and What is the UK? (77) — 149 questions. The app's **Sweep Ch 1 + Ch 2** button loads both at once, or split it into two sittings. Target **90%+**. |
| **24** | **Sweep: History** | On **Practice**, switch on **"Only questions I haven't seen"**, then drill History to confirm full coverage. Target **85%+**. |
| **25** | **Mock #3 + review** | Sit **Mock #3**. Target **22+/24**. Add any new facts to your sheet. |
| **26** | **Mock #4, exam conditions** | Sit **Mock #4** in the **morning**, phone away, no pausing. Target **22+/24** again — you are confirming it now. Then close the last gaps. |
| **27** | **Light revision** | Re-read your key-facts sheet and notes. Official **app** questions for speed. Optionally sit one more full mock (24 questions / 45 minutes). |
| **28** | **Rest and prepare** | No cramming. Confirm your ID and test centre details. Early night. |

## If you fall behind

- **Missed a day?** Do not double up. Move the day's *reading* to the next day and skip
  the extra drills for once — reading first, drilling second, always.
- **Short on time?** Keep the **mock tests** and **Retry wrong**; cut reading of
  Chapter 3's less-tested stretches to a skim.
- **Days 26–28 are non-negotiable:** at least one timed mock and genuine rest.

---

## Test-day checklist

- [ ] Take the **exact same ID** you used to book (original, not a copy or screenshot).
- [ ] Arrive in good time; your photo is taken on the day.
- [ ] **24 questions, 45 minutes, 18/24 (75%) to pass.**
- [ ] Read each question twice — watch for **"Which TWO"** and negatives.
- [ ] Answer everything. There is no penalty for a wrong answer.
- [ ] Flag and move on if stuck; return to it at the end.

## High-yield facts (build your sheet from these)

**Nations & symbols** — Compass: Scotland = Edinburgh, Wales = Cardiff, NI = Belfast,
UK = London. Great Britain = England, Scotland, Wales only. Patron saints: St George
(23 Apr), St Andrew (30 Nov), St David (1 Mar), St Patrick (17 Mar).

**Anchor dates** — AD 43 Roman invasion · AD 410 Romans leave · 1066 Hastings ·
1086 Domesday · 1215 Magna Carta · 1314 Bannockburn · 1348 Black Death · 1415 Agincourt ·
1455 Wars of the Roses begin · 1485 Bosworth · 1530s Reformation · 1588 Armada · 1605 Gunpowder Plot · 1642 Civil War ·
1660 Restoration · 1688–89 Glorious Revolution · 1707 Union with Scotland ·
1801 Union with Ireland (the Act passed in 1800) · 1805 Trafalgar · 1815 Waterloo ·
1922 BBC founded · 1928 full suffrage · 1948 NHS ·
1997 devolution (the institutions themselves were set up in 1999) · 1998 Good Friday
Agreement · 2020 UK leaves the EU (background — the 2013 handbook predates it).

**Institutions** — Monarch = head of state (does not rule) · Commons = elected ·
Lords = not elected · General elections at least every 5 years · Vote from 18 (since 1969) ·
PM lives at 10 Downing Street · Chancellor = economy, Home Secretary = crime/policing/
immigration, Foreign Secretary = abroad · 12-person jury in England/Wales/NI, 15 in
Scotland · the "not proven" verdict was abolished for trials from January 2026.

**Culture** — Shakespeare (plays/sonnets) · Chaucer *Canterbury Tales* ·
*The Mousetrap* (longest-running West End play) · Proms (BBC, since 1927) ·
Wimbledon (oldest tennis tournament) · 1966 World Cup · The Ashes (cricket vs Australia) ·
Turner Prize (art, since 1984) · Booker Prize (fiction, founded 1968, first awarded 1969).

## The shorter plans

The app carries the same schedule at three lengths. Choose one with the selector at
the top of the **Plan** tab; each plan keeps its own ticks, and the app suggests the
longest one that still fits your test date.

| | 4-week (28 days) | 2-week (14 days) | 1-week (7 days) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | One handbook section a day | Two chapters a day | A whole chapter a day (Ch 4 in one) |
| Hours a day | 1–2 | 2–3 | 3–4 |
| History days | 8 (days 4–6, 8–11, 24) | 4 (days 2–5) | 2 (days 2–3) |
| Mock sittings | days 7, 12, 21, 25, 26 | days 5, 9, 11, 12, 13 | days 3, 5, 6, 7 |
| Rest | day 14, plus the light days 27–28 | day 14 | none — day 7 is revision, a mock and an early night |
| Milestones | days 7 / 12 / 21 / 26 | days 5 / 9 / 12 / 13 | days 3 / 5 / 6 / 7 |

All three use the same ladder of scores: a **baseline mock** (~15/24) that you do not
count, then numbered mocks stepping up **18+/24 → 20+/24 → 22+/24**, the last one
under exam conditions.

The **1-week plan is a skim-and-drill schedule, not a read-through.** It assumes you
already know some of the material, so keep the key-facts sheet close and prioritise
the mocks and **Retry wrong** over finishing every reading bullet.

**If your test is more than 4 weeks away,** the 4-week plan finishes before it. Read
the handbook once at your leisure in the meantime and start the plan when you are
about four weeks out.
